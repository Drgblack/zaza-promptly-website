# Positive Reinforcement System

## Steps
1. **Be specific and immediate**: Instead of "good job," say "Thank you for raising your hand and waiting to be called on."
2. **Use a 4:1 ratio**: Four positive comments for every correction. Track this on a clipboard or app for one week.
3. **Vary your reinforcement**: Verbal praise, written notes, privileges, points, or special roles. Different kids respond to different rewards.
4. **Catch them being good**: Actively scan for positive behaviors, especially from students who typically get attention for problems.
5. **Make it public when possible**: "I noticed Marcus helped Sarah find her pencil without being asked. That's the kindness I love to see."
6. **Build peer recognition**: "Turn to someone nearby and tell them one thing they did well in today's lesson."

## Scripts
- "I notice you started your work right away—that shows me you're ready to learn."
- "You showed real persistence when that math problem got tricky. That's what helps brains grow."
- "The way you helped your partner without giving the answer shows excellent teamwork."

## Differentiation
- **SEND**: For students with emotional needs, use private praise or written notes instead of public attention, which may feel overwhelming.
- **ELL**: Use thumbs up, smile, or other non-verbal praise alongside simple, clear language they can understand.
