# Grading Doesn't Have to Steal Your Evenings: A Smarter Way to Give Feedback

*By Dr. Greg Blackburn*

## The Problem: When Grading Becomes Overwhelming

Grading often feels endless. Thirty essays, each needing thoughtful, personalized feedback, can eat up entire evenings. Even when you push through, comments start to sound repetitive.

It's a cycle every teacher knows too well: the stack of papers that grows taller by the day, the sinking feeling when you realize you'll be spending another weekend grading, and the guilt when you know your students deserve better feedback than your exhausted brain can provide.

## The Emotional Impact: What This Really Feels Like

Teachers tell me:

> "By the 20th essay, I'm just repeating myself."

> "I want to be fair to every student, but I'm exhausted."

> "Parents expect detailed feedback, but there aren't enough hours in the day."

This cycle leads to burnout, guilt, and the sense that your best energy is drained by administrative tasks instead of actual teaching. You became a teacher to inspire and educate - not to spend your evenings drowning in paperwork.

The worst part? You know your students deserve better. They deserve thoughtful, personalized feedback that helps them grow. But how can you provide that when you're running on empty?

## Failed Solutions: What Teachers Have Already Tried

### Copy-Paste Templates
Quick, but generic. Students and parents see right through them, and you feel like you're shortchanging your students' individual efforts.

### Detailed Rubrics
Structure helps, but you still need to write unique comments for each student. The time savings are minimal, and the workload remains overwhelming.

### Late Nights and Weekend Marathons
Not sustainable. You pay the price in energy, wellbeing, and family time. This approach leads straight to burnout.
