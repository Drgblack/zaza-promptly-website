# LinkedIn Post: When Grading Feels Like Climbing Everest

---

**For every teacher staring at papers at 10 PM wondering if you'll ever catch up:**

You're not alone, and you're not failing. 

Last week, a teacher posted: "I love my students, but I'm drowning in paperwork. I spent my entire weekend grading, and I still have 40 assignments left. When do I get to be a human being?"

The responses flooded in - hundreds of teachers sharing their own grading marathons, sacrificed weekends, and 2 AM desperation.

Here's what struck me: every single teacher felt like they were the only one struggling.

**But here's the truth - the grading mountain isn't your fault.**

We're expected to give meaningful feedback (correct) while managing impossible class sizes (unrealistic). The math is brutal: 150 students × 3 minutes per paper = 7.5 hours of grading.

**You're not failing the system - the system is failing you.**

## Permission slips for tired teachers:

✅ Grade only what you'll actually use to inform instruction  
✅ Give yourself a grading curfew (8 PM counts as reasonable)  
✅ Use the "traffic light" system: Green (quick check), Yellow (focused feedback), Red (deep feedback)  
✅ Remember that progress matters more than perfection  
✅ Know that your mental health matters more than your gradebook  

Your worth as an educator isn't measured by how many papers you grade or how late you stay up. It's measured by the lives you touch and the minds you inspire.

**Tonight, before you open another paper, ask yourself:**
- Will grading this serve my students' learning?
- Will it inform my next teaching moves?
- Can I do this sustainably?

If the answer is no, close the gradebook. You're not just a teacher - you're a human being who happens to teach. And human beings need rest.
